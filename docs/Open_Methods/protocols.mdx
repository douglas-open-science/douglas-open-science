# Open protocols

## Open protocols: all the details

__Open protocols allow sharing and describing methods more thoroughly than in the methods sections of traditional scientific publications__ – often, the latter have limited space or are relegated to supplementary materials, resulting in protocols that are hard to find, reproduce, and/or adapt. __Open protocols are not exclusive to wet-lab research, as they [pertain to all scientific disciplines](https://dx.doi.org/10.17504/protocols.io.4r3l2odzpv1y/v4) and are also a cornerstone of high-quality study pre-registrations__. Open protocols increase trust with readers and favour new applications of methodologies in their original research field and beyond.

__Open protocols are typically published in dedicated open repositories__ (e.g., [protocols.io](https://www.protocols.io/)) __or as standalone publications__ (e.g., [BMC Psychiatry](https://bmcpsychiatry.biomedcentral.com/submission-guidelines/preparing-your-manuscript) and [JMIR Research Protocols](https://www.researchprotocols.org/)). In either case, __open protocols provide an in-depth description of the steps to follow__, with clear identification of the required materials, tools, and equipment. Some publishing venues allow protocols to be commented and built upon, fostering collaboration and knowledge growth.

:::guide[Open protocols at the Douglas]
At the Douglas, researchers share diverse types of protocols, ranging from [differentiation of pluripotent stem cells](https://doi.org/10.21769/BioProtoc.3188) to [exploring digital stories and video testimonies on mental illness and recovery](https://doi.org/10.2196/33525). The Douglas-Bell Canada Brain Bank has also made available its methods for [post-mortem brain imaging](https://doi.org/10.52294/001c.123347).
:::

## Practical steps
* __Learn best practices for writing open protocols.__ PLOS lists [important points to consider](https://plos.org/resource/how-to-write-your-methods/) when writing protocols. For example, adopt reporting checklists such as [ARRIVE guidelines 2.0](https://arriveguidelines.org/arrive-guidelines) (for animal research), the COBIDAS reporting guidelines (for [MRI](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.4500) and for [M/EEG](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-020-00709-0)) and [MDAR](https://osf.io/bj3mu/) (for authors to compile their methods and for editors and reviewers to verify them). [Keep in mind that straightforward protocols are more likely to be cited and remain relevant](https://theplosblog.plos.org/2021/07/methods-as-scientific-asset/).
* __Compare publishing options for protocols__:
  * Before data collection, protocols can be __pre-registered__ – consult our pre-registration webpage (coming soon!) for additional guidance. 
  * Validated protocols may be published (i) as __research articles__ focused on the validation of the protocol, in scientific journals or (ii) as __protocols on their own__, in scientific journals or open repositories. Both options may also be combined (e.g., [through a partnership between PLOS and protocols.io](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/s/what-we-publish#loc-lab-protocols)). Common publishing venues are:
    * Journals such as [BMC Psychiatry](https://bmcpsychiatry.biomedcentral.com/submission-guidelines/preparing-your-manuscript), [JMIR Research Protocols](https://www.researchprotocols.org/), [bio-protocol](https://bio-protocol.org/), and [PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/s/what-we-publish#loc-lab-protocols). Consult our [open access resource](../Open_Access_Publishing) for guidance on identifying open access publishing routes and journals.
    * [__protocols.io__](https://www.protocols.io/), a platform for developing and sharing reproducible methods in all disciplines. Their interactive platform allows following protocols and recording changes during experiments, as well as discussing and improving protocols after publication. Submissions receive DOIs and are not peer-reviewed, but can be coupled to scientific peer-reviewed publications. Consult here [examples of protocols](https://www.protocols.io/view/examples-of-protocols-on-protocols-io-from-differe-4r3l2odzpv1y/v2) and a detailed [FAQ](https://www.protocols.io/help/faq) section.
    *	__Generalist repositories__ such as [Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/).
* __Adopt persistent identifiers when writing open protocols__. DOIs can be assigned to the protocol itself and RRIDs can clearly identify materials, equipment or core facilities. Consult our [guidance](../Persistent_Identifiers/index.mdx) on persistent identifiers and learn more about [persistent identifiers relevant to the Douglas community](../Persistent_Identifiers/other-applications#pids-for-research-resources). 
* __Attribute open licenses to open protocols where possible__ but note that [certain types of methods may not be copyrightable](https://www.protocols.io/help/faq#q-10). Consult our open licenses webpage (coming soon!) for additional guidance.
