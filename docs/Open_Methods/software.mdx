# Open software

__Open software can be reused and shared as part of research projects.__ Typically, the source code of open software is [available to be viewed, copied, learnt from, altered or shared by others](https://opensource.com/resources/what-open-source). Like other types of open research outputs (e.g., open access publishing), open software [exists on a spectrum](https://opensciency.github.io/sprint-content/open-software/lesson1-introduction.html), mostly dictated by the types of open licenses (coming soon!) applied to it and the resulting limitations on downstream applications.

__Sharing open software largely benefits the research community__. When you enable other researchers to collect or process their data using the same code as you, they will be spared of rewriting your software or purchasing commercial alternatives: __your work will have greater impact and will also save others time and resources__. Yet, downstream applications of open software are not limited to simple reuse, as __open code can be modified and built upon__, leading to enhanced innovation and discovery. 

__Open software increases research transparency__, allowing for understanding of computational methods in detail, identifying and correcting mistakes, and assessing the reproducibility and replicability of findings.

:::guide[Open software at the Douglas]
At the Douglas, open software takes many shapes and forms, ranging from [fMRI processing pipelines for rodents](https://github.com/CoBrALab/RABIES) and [open neuroimaging tasks](https://portal.conp.ca/experiments/) to [pipelines for calculating expression-based polygenetic risk scores](https://portal.conp.ca/pipeline?id=zenodo.4685127). The [Douglas Neuroinformatics Platform](https://douglas.research.mcgill.ca/neuroinformatics-platform/) is also [based on open software](https://github.com/DouglasNeuroInformatics).

## Practical steps
* __Adopt open software throughout your research process__. Popular tools include [Python](https://www.python.org/) (data analysis), [LaTex](https://latex-tutorial.com/) (typeset), [ImageJ](https://imagej.net/ij/) (image processing), and [R](https://www.r-project.org/) and [JASP](https://jasp-stats.org/) (statistical analysis). Field-specific open software (e.g., for neuroimaging) are also often available and are often mentioned in papers or can be learned about through colleagues. Diverse resources also include [open source software for project management and communications](https://opensource.com/alternatives). 
* __When reusing open software, respect the copyright and license terms of others__. If modifying the original code, release the new version as open software if possible. 
* __Learn best practices for developing open software__, such as annotating code to enhance understandability. Improve your coding skills through [The Carpentries](https://carpentries.org/) or [other training resources](https://www.mcgill.ca/drs/training). Consult guidance on [software management plans](https://zenodo.org/record/2159713#.Yy_uUaRBxRY), [writing reproducible code](https://bvreede.github.io/presentations/presentations/2020-09-17_BestPractices-lecture.html#1), and [software security](https://openssf.org/blog/2023/09/06/strengthening-open-source-software-best-practices-for-enhanced-security/). 
* __Discover [other ways to support open software without writing code](https://opensource.com/life/13/10/ten-ways-open-source-projects).__ For example, you can [report bugs, contribute tutorials, or write documentation for existing tools](https://opensource.guide/how-to-contribute/).
* __Respect ethics frameworks when sharing open software__. Sometimes software designed for research projects contains data from individual participants: this data must not be included when sharing the software unless ethically approved. Contact the [Research Support and Framework Unit](https://www.ciusss-ouestmtl.gouv.qc.ca/en/n-research-teaching-and-innovation/research/research-support-and-framework-unit-user) from the MWI-Island IUHSSC for questions related to ethics of data sharing.
* __Find and share open software through the appropriate repositories and platforms__. Generalist repositories such as [GitHub](https://github.com/) or [GitLab](https://gitlab.com/) are common sharing venues – for more details, consult a [comparison of repositories](https://openresearchsoftware.metajnl.com/about) by the Journal of Open Research Software. Open Neuroscience also provides a [curated list of computational tools for mental health](https://open-neuroscience.com/en/). Other platforms may offer options for sharing software specific to your needs:
  * __Find, create or share experiments, such as behavioural, cognitive, and neuroimaging tasks__, through platforms such as [CONP Experiments](https://portal.conp.ca/experiments/) (supported by the 2022 [Douglas Open Science Awards](https://douglas.research.mcgill.ca/douglas-open-science-awards/)), [Pavlovia](https://pavlovia.org/), [Experiment Factory](http://www.expfactory.org/), [PsychoPy](https://www.psychopy.org/#free), and [OpenSesame](https://osdoc.cogsci.nl/).
  * __Create executable and reproducible publications__ through [NeuroLibre](https://neurolibre.org/).
* __Attribute open licenses to open software and accompanying documentation.__ Consult guidance from our open license webpage (coming soon!) and McGill’s P[olicy on Inventions and Software](https://www.mcgill.ca/secretariat/files/secretariat/policy_on_inventions_and_software.pdf). Learn about open license options for software with the [choose an open source license](https://choosealicense.com/) tool or lists by the [Free Software Foundation](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/license-list#SoftwareLicenses) and the [Open Source Initiative](https://opensource.org/licenses). 
* __Assign persistent identifiers to open software__. Consult our [persistent identifiers resource](../Persistent_Identifiers) for guidance and know that there are workarounds for repositories that do not attribute DOIs (e.g., [Zenodo GitHub integrations](https://docs.github.com/en/repositories/archiving-a-github-repository/referencing-and-citing-content)). Verify how the chosen repository handles versioning (e.g., see [here](https://help.zenodo.org/faq/#versioning) for Zenodo).
* __Link your open software to your webpage, CV, and [ORCID](../Persistent_Identifiers/orcid)__. This will promote your open software contributions and increase their findability. 
* __Join or create open software communities__. The Open Source Initiative organizes [events](https://opensource.org/events) and lists [several organizations supporting open software](https://opensource.com/resources/organizations), while the International Neuroinformatics Coordinating Facility (INCF) hosts [working groups](http://incf.org/resources/working-groups) focused on neuroscience. Consult the Open Source Way [guidebook](https://www.theopensourceway.org/) to learn to grow and manage open code communities.
